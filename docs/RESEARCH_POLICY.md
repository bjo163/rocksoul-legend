# Research Policy

## Source priority

1. primary physical evidence;
2. archaeological / epigraphic records;
3. contemporary documentary evidence;
4. early historical sources;
5. peer-reviewed scholarship;
6. university press;
7. museums, archives, and libraries;
8. recognized institutions;
9. popular secondary material for discovery only.

## Guardrails

- Evidence before interpretation.
- Correlation is not causation.
- Similarity is not transmission.
- A later source is not contemporary evidence.
- Tradition is evidence that a tradition exists, not automatic evidence the narrated event occurred.
- Preserve conflicting scholarship.
- Never fabricate or autocomplete citations.
- Keep event existence separate from event interpretation.
- Keep uncertainty as structured data.
- Prefer a weaker relation over unsupported causal language.

Strong relations such as `caused`, `origin_of`, and `inspired` require explicit evidence.
Default cross-narrative status is `hypothesis`.
